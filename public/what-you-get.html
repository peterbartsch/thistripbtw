What's free, what you pay for

Building a trip costs nothing. Paying is what makes it last, and shareable.

There is no free tier and no trial. Those are different things, and the difference matters. A trial is a countdown. This is simpler: you can build a whole trip — every stop, every leg, the real map — without an account, a card, or an email address. What you're paying for is an address that stays put for years and links you can hand to people.

Before you pay anything

  • Build the whole trip. Add stops, set dates, chain the legs, watch the route draw along real roads.
  • No account, no card, no email. Not asked for, not optional-but-nagged. Not collected.
  • Ask can start it for you. Type “drive from Denver to Moab on the 4th” and it builds the legs — two turns per draft before you buy.
  • Open the demo and click around a finished trip, no strings.

The catch, stated plainly: until you buy, the draft lives in your browser and nowhere else. Clear your data, use private browsing, or pick up a different phone, and it's gone. There is no copy on our side, because we never asked who you are. Buying is what moves it somewhere it can survive.

Why the free part is free

Because a planner you can't try is a planner nobody tries. Asking for a card before you know whether the thing works is how you end up with a signup funnel instead of a product.

And because there's nothing to harvest. A free tier normally has to earn its keep some other way — ads, data, an upsell that follows you around. We take a few dollars once, so the free part can just be free.

The account is made for you, and it comes after

Buying never sends you through a signup form — you never fill in one to buy something. Afterwards, we make you an account from the email Stripe already has for your receipt, so your trips are findable later. Setting a password is optional: without one, we'll email you a link to set one whenever you need it. Your trip links keep working either way — they never needed an account.

An account holds three things: your email, a one-way hash of your password, and the list of your trips. That's it — no name, no profile, nothing about what you did.

Worth knowing either way: opening a trip still asks for its phrase, and we only ever stored a one-way hash of that. So an account remembers which trips are yours, not how to get into them. Keep the edit link somewhere you'll find it.

The people you share a trip with don't need an account at all. The link is the key.
