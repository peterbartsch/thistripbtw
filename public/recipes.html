What to say

Sentences that get an assistant to build the trip, and the links they produce.

If your assistant is connected to this service, these are the things worth saying. Every link below is real — each one was produced by the tool it names, and opens on a map you can change. There is no sign-up to build one and no card until you decide to keep it.

Not connected yet? The one-line setup is on the page for assistants. Nothing here needs an API key.

Start it

build_trip_link

drive from Denver to Moab on Friday, then home Monday

The shortest thing that works. An origin and one destination is enough — the assistant looks up both places, builds the link, and hands it over. Dates are optional: an undated leg keeps its place in the order.

build_trip_link

Mel drives Phoenix to Vegas on the 9th, Sam flies in, we both fly to SF on the 12th

Two people, two ways of travelling, one map. Say who is on which leg and the trip draws each path in its own colour — this is the shape the product was built for, and the one other planners handle worst.

Open that trip →

find_place

where exactly is RNO?

Turns a name, an airport code or a trailhead into coordinates, and hands back up to six candidates so the right Reno gets picked. The assistant will not guess a coordinate — a wrong guess puts your trip in the wrong country.

Change it

amend_trip_link

add two nights in Sedona before Vegas

Amending rebuilds the same trip with the change in it and gives you the new link. Say it as many times as the plan moves — one trip grows through the whole conversation instead of arriving finished and wrong.

read_trip_link

what's on this trip? <paste a link>

Reads a link back into an itinerary — in a new conversation, next month, or by a different assistant entirely. The link is the record: the trip rides inside it, so nothing was stored anywhere to be looked up.

After you keep it

add_to_kept_trip

add the taco place we found to the trip

A kept trip lives at its own address, and the link you were given is the password to it. An assistant holding that link can add a stop on your behalf while you are standing in front of the thing. Nothing is ever deleted this way — there is no delete tool at all, because only the person who bought a trip should be able to destroy it.

read_kept_trip

what did we put on the Tahoe trip?

Reads a kept trip back, with the stops in order, who was on which leg, and the notes. Treat the link like a password — anyone holding it can read the trip.

Two things it will not do

It will not invent a departure airport, a flight time or a coordinate. If it cannot look something up, it says so and asks. A confident wrong answer is worse than a question on the one plan you are going to drive.

It will not put anything in the link you did not say. A trip link carries its contents in the open, so keep passport numbers, home addresses and phone numbers out of names and notes — and tell your assistant the same if it offers.
